How to Convert YouTube Subtitles to Text (Step-by-Step Guide)

Have you ever watched a YouTube video and wanted to copy its subtitles or captions as text? Whether you're a student, content creator, or researcher, converting YouTube subtitles to text can save you hours of manual typing. Within seconds, you can turn any video's speech into a readable document.

What Are YouTube Subtitles?

YouTube subtitles (also called captions) are the written version of the audio in a video. They are designed to help viewers understand content better, especially when watching without sound or in a foreign language. These captions can be either manually uploaded by the creator or auto-generated by YouTube's AI.

💡 Pro Tip: Turning videos into text is a secret productivity hack. You can convert an educational tutorial into a full blog post or study guide without re-watching the video multiple times!

Step-by-Step: Convert YouTube Subtitles to Text

If you want to extract text quickly and professionally, follow these simple steps:

  1. Open the Video: Go to the YouTube video that has captions enabled.
  2. Copy the URL: Copy the link from your browser's address bar.
  3. Use an Extractor: Paste the link into our YouTube subtitles to text converter.
  4. Extract: Click the convert button and wait for the AI to process the captions.
  5. Save: Copy the plain text or download it as a document.

Alternative Method (Manual Way)

If you prefer not to use a tool, YouTube has a built-in feature, though it is a bit slower:

  • Click on the three dots (...) below the video player.
  • Select "Show transcript" from the menu.
  • The transcript will appear on the right side; you can then highlight and copy the text manually.

👉 Note: This method often includes timestamps which can be annoying to remove manually.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal to convert YouTube subtitles to text?
Yes, it is perfectly legal for personal use, study, or research. However, always credit the original creator if you plan to use the text in your own public content.

Can I convert auto-generated subtitles?
Absolutely. Most tools can fetch both "CC" (Closed Captions) and auto-generated transcripts.
